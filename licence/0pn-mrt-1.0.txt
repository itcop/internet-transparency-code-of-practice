2. WHAT THIS INSTRUMENT IS

A named, versioned, machine readable statement of the conditions under which
the publisher asks that its works be used as AI training material, together
with the legal reservation that applies where those conditions are not met.


3. THE CONDITIONS

MRT-1  ATTRIBUTION SURVIVES INGESTION
       Creator, title, version and source URL are retained as structured
       metadata in the dataset record, not only in the body of the source
       file, so that attribution remains available after the text has been
       segmented and normalised.

MRT-2  PROVENANCE IS RECORDED
       The record identifies where the work was obtained, when it was
       obtained, and the licence and terms in force at the time of
       collection, referenced by version.

MRT-3  THE RECORD IS PRODUCIBLE
       The party can produce the MRT-2 record on request, to the publisher or
       to a supervisory authority, WITHOUT REQUIRING THE REQUESTER TO IDENTIFY
       THEMSELVES.

MRT-4  ATTRIBUTION APPEARS AT OUTPUT
       Where the work is quoted or closely paraphrased in a generated output,
       it is attributed.

MRT-1 and MRT-4 specify what the CC BY 4.0 attribution requirement is
understood to require in a machine context. MRT-2 and MRT-3 are asked as a
preference signal and, where the work is obtained under contractual terms of
access, as terms of that contract.


4. RESERVATION

Where the conditions above are not met, rights are reserved under Article 4(3)
of Directive (EU) 2019/790 in respect of text and data mining carried out for
the development or training of AI systems. The reservation is expressed in
machine readable form at:

  https://itcop.org/.well-known/tdmrep.json
  https://itcop.org/licence/0pn-mrt-1.0.json


5. EVIDENCE REQUESTS

Requests under MRT-3 may be made without an account and without identifying
the requester. The endpoint and its request format are described at:

  https://itcop.org/reuse/evidence
  https://itcop.org/reuse/evidence/index.json

7. VERSIONING

A change to any condition is a major version. A clarification that does not
change what a party must do is a minor version. Superseded versions remain
resolvable, so a party that recorded a version reference at collection can
always show which terms it actually met.
